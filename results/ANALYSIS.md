# Analysis of the second-pass experiment

Evidence: [`benchmark_runs.csv`](benchmark_runs.csv), 3,600 runs, and [`benchmark_progress.csv`](benchmark_progress.csv), 374,400 actual target executions. `scripts/validate_results.py` checked both files. The [generated summary](summary.md) reports mean, median, sample standard deviation, range, and normal 95% mean interval for every condition; [plots](plots/) are generated from the raw CSVs. GCC 13.3.0, C++20 Release with `-O3 -DNDEBUG`, six deterministic eight-bit targets, five policies, 30 seed indices, budgets 32/64/128/192 distinct executions. Paper seed offsets `k=0,1,2` are used for DCF, random mutation, and uniform sampling; each ablation uses its parent policy's offset. Previous data are isolated in [legacy](legacy/).

## Dependency discovery per target execution

On the paper's `and3` target, DCF found a mean **2.967 of three** dependencies by 64 executions; random mutation found **1.867**, and uniform sampling **0.933**. The DCF–random difference of means is 1.100 edges with a normal 95% interval [0.772, 1.428] across independent randomized streams on this fixed target. The normalized area under the execution-coverage curve was 0.553 for DCF, 0.254 for random, and 0.056 for uniform, so DCF also found those edges earlier. At budget 128 all three policies found all three edges in all 30 runs. Random therefore converged to the same final coverage on this small 256-input target; the early advantage did not persist in final coverage at saturation.

The same-mutator `mutation_feedback` ablation found 2.900/3 edges and area 0.489 at budget 64, compared with random's 1.867/3 and 0.254. This is evidence that choosing parents from dependency discoverers helps this target under the shared mutation operator. The paper's complete DCF policy also constructs quartets, so its direct advantage cannot be assigned wholly to corpus feedback. The `fixed_parent` quartet ablation found 3.000/3 edges and area 0.582 at 64, slightly above DCF's 2.967 and 0.553. These runs do not show an added benefit from discoverer-parent selection inside the quartet scheduler on `and3`.

Other structures differ. On `nested_bug`, DCF found 1.900/4 edges at 64 versus random's 1.367/4, with coverage area 0.241 versus 0.125. At 128, random's final mean was higher (3.833 versus 3.400) while DCF's area remained slightly higher (0.434 versus 0.411). On `rare_bug` at 128, DCF found 1.233/28, random 1.933/28, and uniform 2.567/28. At 192, random found 8.900/28 and uniform 10.633/28 versus DCF's 5.800/28. The DCF–uniform difference of means at 192 was -4.833 edges, normal 95% interval [-7.521, -2.146]. Under this rare full-input condition, broader sampling won at the tested budget. The bug outcome creates many valid witnesses only in narrow contexts, so this is a difficult target for focused quartet requests.

Constant, direct-output, and independent-output controls had **zero** reference and observed dependencies in every run. At budget 64, DCF nevertheless saw a mean 7.67 distinct outcomes on `direct` and 7.77 on `independent`. All measured runs had zero false edges relative to exhaustive enumeration. This confirms that the detector does not equate an arbitrary output change with Equation (2) coverage on these deterministic targets. It does not establish reliability for noisy targets.

## Wall-clock discovery and throughput

In this recorded run, `and3` at budget 64 took a mean 88.7 µs for DCF, 96.7 µs for random, and 94.4 µs for uniform. Mean throughputs were 723,103, 663,704, and 678,701 executions/s. The independent-stream DCF–random difference of mean runtime was -8.0 µs, normal 95% interval [-10.3, -5.7]. DCF found more dependencies both per execution and per measured second **on this run and target**. These microsecond timings should not be extrapolated to real target programs. Paths were warmed and policy order rotated, but host load, clock overhead, and an unusually cheap in-process oracle can dominate.

At `rare_bug` budget 128, DCF's mean throughput was 887,222 executions/s versus random's 800,004, yet random found more dependencies by the same execution budget. On `direct` at budget 64, DCF's runtime was lower than random's (76.1 versus 83.8 µs) despite zero discoverable dependencies for either policy. Algorithmic discovery per execution and practical throughput are distinct outcomes; lower runtime alone does not imply more useful exploration.

## Bug discovery, separately from coverage

No general DCF bug advantage is supported. On `nested_bug` at 64 executions, the one known bug was found in **14/30** DCF runs, **21/30** random runs, and **29/30** uniform runs. At 128 the counts were 29/30, 30/30, and 30/30. On `rare_bug`, at 128 they were 11/30, 12/30, and 14/30; at 192 they were 20/30, 23/30, and 26/30. `mutation_feedback` found the rare bug in only 1/30 runs at 128 despite finding the direct AND dependency in all runs. At `nested_bug` budget 64, DCF's dependency area (0.241) exceeded uniform's (0.037), while uniform found the bug in more runs. Thus increased Dependency Coverage did not imply increased bug success in these data.

The raw run CSV leaves first-bug execution and elapsed time blank when the event was not reached. Success rates always use all 30 trials. Event-time summaries describe successful runs only **and** state the censored count; failures are never silently removed from success-rate denominators. Each bug target has one explicit bug condition, not multiple crash classes.

## Feedback cost and limits

At `and3` budget 64, DCF's measured aggregate target-call time was 2.0 µs and detector insertion time 75.8 µs within an 88.7 µs full run. Random's corresponding figures were 1.9, 82.2, and 96.7 µs. Residual time includes proposal generation, cache lookup, scheduling, progress recording, and timer calls. Because **all** policies use the detector to measure the same metric, this experiment does **not** estimate the extra cost of adding dependency tracking to a detector-free random fuzzer. It does show that detector work dominates these very cheap synthetic oracles. `mutation_feedback` made many duplicate proposals from its small corpus near saturation; on `rare_bug` at 192 its mean runtime was 450.0 µs versus DCF's 190.9 µs. This is an ablation limitation, not a paper DCF result.

The tested conclusion is narrow: controlled queries efficiently find the paper's short, separate AND interactions, while random search catches up in this finite space. The signal contributes little on zero-interaction controls and can lose to broad random sampling for a rare complete assignment. Differences vary with seed and target; 30 runs on six synthetic functions do not quantify variability across real programs. The indexed arrow is an observational witness, not proof of internal causality. No code coverage, AFL, stateful-service, noisy-target, or real vulnerability claim is supported here.
